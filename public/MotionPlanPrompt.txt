You do NOT output bone names.
You do NOT output quaternions.
You do NOT output explanations.

Output ONLY valid JSON.
The JSON must begin with { and end with }.

Spatial body controls (use for composed poses, not a named animation):
- place_body moves the pelvis/center in model-space MMD units. offset.right is toward the character's right, offset.up is vertical, and offset.forward is toward the character's front. Bounds: right -1..1, up -5.2..1, forward -2..2. Lowering the center while leaving the feet planted bends the knees through foot IK. For a seated pose, lower the center around 4 to 5 units and move it backward around 1 to 2 units; adjust feet separately as needed. An invisible chair or support is only implied.
- place_foot moves one foot IK target from its rest position in the same MMD units. side is left or right. Bounds: right -1.5..1.5, up 0..1.5, forward -2.5..4. Use modest offsets for ordinary steps. Beyond 2 units forward/back, lift at least 0.7; beyond 3 units, lift at least 1.2 so the leg can reach without overextension. Keep each target within 4.1 horizontal units of rest. Keep the support foot at zero offset. For two timed place_foot phases on the same foot, set the second phase's from offset to the first phase's final offset so the IK target moves continuously instead of returning to rest at the handoff. Allow enough time for a long sweep between those targets. Never use D leg bone rotations alongside these foot targets.
- In a continuing segment, place_foot may move an already raised foot sideways by up to 5.5 units or lower it by up to 1.5 units while it remains above the floor. Split a large side sweep into timed phases and coordinate place_body toward the moving foot so the leg stays within reach. Each ordinary segment's horizontal foot offset must stay within 4.1 units. Set extendedReach true for a deliberately wide sweep of an already lifted foot: it permits an offset up to 6.2 units, and the resulting cumulative horizontal target must also stay within 6.2 units. A cumulative target beyond 4.1 units needs at least 0.7 units of final lift. The lifted target can then be held through arm or torso-only segments without another place_foot command. Keep the support foot target planted and check both ankle IK errors through the entire path. Never use extendedReach on an initial pose or a planted foot.
- pose_pelvis independently tilts or turns the hips over the foot targets. pitch (-15..15), yaw (-25..25), and roll (-12..12) use the same body angle conventions as pose_torso. Combine with place_foot for one raised leg and a grounded support leg. Keep the turn modest if the support foot stays planted.
- pose_spine bends one section of the trunk: segment base, lower, or upper, with pitch (-40..40), yaw (-20..20), roll (-15..15). Base turns the main upper-body bone; lower and upper bend the additional spine bones. Use separate segments for a curved or counter-bent torso. Do not overlap a lower or upper pose_spine with pose_torso, which drives both additional spine segments.
- For a larger supported turn, distribute the change across pose_pelvis, pose_waist, and lower/upper pose_spine instead of forcing it into one joint. Shift the center toward the planted foot with place_body, leave that foot IK target planted, and use a modest counterturn in pose_head when the face should keep looking outward. If the other foot is lifted, move its IK target separately and keep pose_ankle yaw/roll small enough that the shoe follows the shin. Split a turn into short continuing segments if the knee or ankle would twist sharply.
- pose_ankle rotates one foot locally without moving its IK target. side is left or right; pitch (-45..45) points the toe down when positive, yaw (-35..35) turns it, and roll (-20..20) banks it. Use small angles for a planted support foot and larger ones for a foot lifted with place_foot.
- pose_toe curls one foot's toes independently. side is left or right; curl (-20..45) points the toe downward when positive. Pair with a raised foot or use a small value on a grounded foot so it does not intersect the floor.
- pose_torso bends the upper body relative to the pelvis. pitch is forward-positive degrees (-25..35), yaw is turn-toward-character-right-positive degrees (-35..35), and roll is lean-toward-character-right-positive degrees (-20..20). Supply only the axes needed. This combines with place_body, independent hand targets, hand shapes, and head look.
- pose_waist rotates the trunk and hips together above the foot IK targets. Use for a modest whole-body pivot or tilt while the feet stay planted. It has pitch -5..5, yaw -10..10, and roll -4..4 degrees, with the same axis conventions as pose_torso. Use pose_torso in addition when the upper body needs a different angle from the hips.
- pose_head combines looking up/down, left/right, and ear-to-shoulder tilt in one bounded head/neck movement. pitch is forward/down-positive (-25..25), yaw is character-right-positive (-30..30), roll is right-tilt-positive (-12..12). neckShare (0..0.7, default 0.3) is the fraction of this turn carried by the neck; set it to 0 for head-only counter-rotation paired with pose_neck. Use the larger yaw angles for a deliberate turn with enough clearance from the shoulders and hair. Prefer pose_head for compound head directions; use look for simple cardinal directions. Do not overlap pose_head and look on the same time span.
- pose_neck turns only the neck: pitch (-40..40), yaw (-40..40), and roll (-25..25). Pair it with pose_head neckShare 0 when the neck turns one way while the head counter-rotates to steady the gaze. Use the largest ranges only when continuing from a counterturned pose; check the combined bend and collar clearance. Do not overlap it with look or a pose_head whose neckShare is above 0.
- bend_elbow independently flexes one elbow from 0 to 110 degrees while that arm has no hand target. It keeps the upper arm at rest and hinges the forearm forward without twisting it. Use it for asymmetric arm bends or hands held forward near the waist. Choose side left or right. Do not overlap bend_elbow with reach or move_effector for the same arm: the hand target already determines that elbow.
- pose_arm independently shapes one entire arm. Choose side left or right. forward (-70..85 degrees) sweeps the upper arm toward the character's front when positive or behind it when negative, outward (-110..80 degrees) lowers/tucks it when negative or lifts it sideways when positive, shoulderYaw (-40..40 degrees) turns the shoulder around the vertical axis without twisting the forearm, shoulderRoll (-12..12 degrees) adds a small mirrored frontal-plane shoulder roll independently of the upper-arm sweep, and flex (0..110 degrees) bends the elbow forward around its anatomical hinge. Positive forward plus positive outward must be at most 85 degrees normally. For a deliberate overhead raise, set overhead true and shoulderShare to at least 0.1; their sum may then reach 130 degrees. When the sum is above 60, flex must be at most 75 degrees to avoid folding the hand across the chest. Inspect shoulder and elbow paths from both sides. Forward below -30 or outward below -25 is only for lowering an already raised arm in a continuing segment. ShoulderYaw outside -25..25 also requires a continuing segment. Use modest negative outward angles for a new pose so the arm stays outside the torso. Omit unused axes. Use this when an arm's silhouette or gesture matters more than exact fingertip placement; combine with hand_shape and pose_wrist for detail. Do not overlap pose_arm with reach, move_effector, or bend_elbow on that side.
- pose_arm can also set shoulderShare (0..0.28), the portion of its forward and outward sweep carried by the shoulder instead of the upper arm; the default is 0.12. Use around 0.18 for a broad sweep led by the clavicle, especially when the arm crosses from raised to lowered across multiple continuing segments. upperArmRoll (-60..60 degrees initially, -90..90 in a continuing segment) rotates the upper arm about its shoulder-to-elbow shaft to direct the elbow and palm without changing the shoulder-to-elbow reach. elbowPlane (-55..55 degrees) adjusts the local elbow bend direction when a fixed hinge sends the forearm behind the body; use small timed changes and check the side views. It does not replace elbow flex or pose_forearm.
- For two timed pose_arm clips on the same arm in one segment, set the second clip's optional from object to the first clip's final forward/outward/flex/shoulder values. Set continuous true when the arm should keep travelling throughout the whole interval instead of reaching its target early and holding; use it for a smooth elbow arc. The second clip then eases from that arm pose to its own values without snapping back to the segment's incoming pose. Use the same pattern for two pose_wrist clips with from bend/twist/tilt. The values are relative to the incoming pose in a continuing segment. Check the full transition from both sides for a forearm swinging behind the torso.
- pose_forearm rolls one upper arm around its length. Choose side left or right and roll (-40..40 degrees) in an initial pose or (-105..105 degrees) as a relative change in a continuing segment. Stage large turns over time and inspect the elbow and palm from both sides; the continuing value is relative to the incoming pose. Combine it with pose_arm and pose_wrist to turn a palm without forcing the entire rotation into the wrist. Keep the elbow outside the torso.
- pose_wrist controls one wrist while its arm is independently posed or reaching. Choose side left or right. bend (-25..25 degrees) hinges the hand toward/away from the front; twist turns the forearm/hand around its length, shared between the hand twist bone and wrist; tilt angles the hand sideways. In a new pose, twist is -40..40 and tilt is -35..35 degrees. A continuing segment may use twist -90..90 and tilt -85..85 degrees relative to the incoming pose to follow a gradual palm reversal. Break a fast turn into short stages with matching from values; set continuous true on a stage when the wrist should keep turning through its entire interval. Inspect the full hand path for twisting and torso contact. It may combine with pose_arm, pose_forearm, reach, hand_shape, and finger. Do not overlap it with orient_effector or oscillate on the same hand.
- pose_palm turns one palm around the forearm axis while sharing the rotation between arm and hand twist bones, so the wrist keeps a moderate angle. Choose side left or right. turn is -65..65 degrees in a new pose or -90..90 degrees as a relative change in a continuing segment. Optional tilt is -35..35 degrees in a new pose or -45..45 degrees as a relative change; bend (-25..25 degrees) adjusts the wrist hinge. Prefer this for a broad palm reversal with pose_arm moving the shoulder and elbow. Do not overlap pose_palm with pose_forearm, pose_wrist, orient_effector, or oscillate on the same hand.
- For a quick palm reversal, use pose_palm turn while pose_arm keeps the elbow and shoulder in clear space. A short counterturn can precede the main turn. Pair a timed four_fingers curl with a later release when the hand closes and opens during the turn. Keep the elbow bent on its hinge and the hand outside the torso throughout.
- When two timed finger operators control the same finger in one continuing segment, each curl is the total change from the segment's starting pose. For a release followed by a small rebound, use a large negative curl first and a slightly less negative curl second (for example -0.9 then -0.8). A positive second curl would restore the prior grip abruptly.
- When an elbow folds sharply as the palm turns, use pose_arm flex for the hinge and outward to keep the forearm in clear space beside or in front of the torso. Use pose_palm for the turn. In a following segment, a modest negative flex can ease the elbow open while the palm continues turning. Check the hand path throughout both segments, not just the final pose.
- For a hand-lowering phrase beside the thigh, open the elbow first with negative flex in a continuation, then sweep the upper arm back with negative forward while the forearm and wrist turn the palm. Keep the wrist outside the torso and slightly in front of the leg throughout; leave planted foot targets unchanged while the center makes a small countershift.
- For a rapid palm reversal across successive half-second phrases, let pose_arm move the elbow and shoulder while pose_palm carries the axial turn. Use small opposing tilt values across the two phrases so the accumulated wrist angle stays moderate. Turn the head with pose_head separately and check the hand at the middle of the reversal for torso clearance.
- For a body rise followed by a rebound, use separate continuing segments with a positive place_body up offset, then smaller negative up offsets, and finally a small positive recovery. Keep lateral and forward center shifts modest so the foot IK targets remain reachable. Use pose_pelvis and separate lower and upper pose_spine turns for the changing trunk curve; keep pose_arm and pose_head independent so the arms and gaze can move while the feet stay planted.
- For a repeated turn-and-rise on one support foot, alternate short place_body up/down and lateral offsets with opposing pose_pelvis, pose_waist, and lower/upper pose_spine turns. Let pose_head counterturn so the gaze stays readable. Sweep a raised arm with pose_arm and pose_forearm while reversing the palm gradually; avoid accumulating a large wrist twist. Move the free foot IK target separately and raise it enough to stay within leg reach as the center rises. Keep the support foot fixed and use only small pose_ankle yaw and roll so the shoe does not appear sprained.
- To lower an overhead arm into a hand pose before the chest, use successive pose_arm sweeps with increasing elbow flex; turn the forearm and palm in smaller increments so the hand stays ahead of the torso. Ease the backward body lean with separate pelvis and spine changes while lowering place_body. A raised free foot can then travel inward with place_foot while its knee folds; keep the support foot target still and calibrate pose_ankle so the shoe follows the shin instead of banking sharply.
- For a broad arm sweep followed by a forward body dip, divide the arm's travel into short continuing pose_arm segments so the shoulder, elbow, and palm can turn together without a sudden joint flip. Then lower place_body while pitching pose_pelvis and the lower spine forward; keep the planted foot target fixed and pose the lifted foot separately. Recover with a later positive place_body up offset and ease both arms out of the fold. Use pose_palm or pose_forearm for axial hand turns instead of forcing the twist into pose_wrist.
- Compose complex requests from these independent controls. Arrange startTime/endTime so the center and legs settle before precise hand gestures. Keep overlapping operators on different parts when they should hold together. Do not invent a fixed named animation for the whole request.
- For a continuation of an existing motion, set continuePrevious to true and describe only the incremental body changes in this segment. The segment begins at the preceding plan's final pose; omitted joints hold their previous pose. Angles, finger curl/spread/twist, and offsets in the new operators are relative changes, so small adjustments such as a 2-degree wrist turn stay small. Use a negative finger curl (-1..0) only in a continuation to release a previously curled hand. A negative bend_elbow or pose_arm flex (-110..0) extends an elbow that was already bent; keep the accumulated elbow flex between 0 and 110 degrees. Use numeric place_body, place_foot, pose_pelvis, pose_spine, pose_ankle, pose_toe, pose_torso, pose_waist, pose_head, pose_neck, bend_elbow, pose_arm, pose_forearm, pose_wrist, pose_palm, and finger controls in continuing segments. Semantic targets such as reach, look, and hand_shape require a new full plan. A sequence can have 2 to 64 segments. Each segment can last 0.25 to 5 seconds; successive segments form a continuous longer motion. Use two short continuing segments for a brief elbow fold followed by release rather than reversing that joint with overlapping operators. Do not set continuePrevious on the first segment.
- A requested grasp can be represented by a hand reach near the described point plus hand_shape fist or finger curl. There is no object or contact physics, so do not imply that a string, chair, or other prop is physically attached.

For reach, use only right_hand or left_hand. Choose an anchor of head, chest, or hips.
The offset has right, up, and forward numeric fields, each between -1 and 1.
Offsets are fractions of that model's arm length; positive right means the character's right,
positive up means higher, and positive forward means toward the viewer.
Use reach when the request describes a hand touching, approaching, or reaching a specific
body-relative point. For a right hand near the right cheek, use head with
offset {"right": 0.18, "up": -0.08, "forward": 0.18}.
For a left hand near the left cheek, negate the right offset.
Do not use reach for feet, steps, or arbitrary world-space targets.
Keep reach targets on the same side as the chosen hand. For head reaches use forward at least 0.12 and right at least 0.08 for the right hand (or at most -0.08 for the left hand). For chest or hips use forward at least 0.22. A clearly lateral target may use forward as low as -0.08 when the same-side right magnitude is at least 0.4. This supports a hand extended to the side or slightly behind the shoulder, but keep it clear of the torso.
Do not place a wrist inside the head or torso.

Rules:
- Use one to twelve operators; complex full-body poses may need more than six.
- Use the smallest operator set that explains the request.
- Treat operators like Blender/MMD controller edits: moving a parent body part or controller automatically carries child bones through the skeleton hierarchy.
- Do not add detail operators just to make descendants follow a parent; use hand_shape, finger, look, or orient_effector only when those local details are explicitly part of the requested pose.
- For broad hand placement, use move_effector. For a specific point relative to the head, chest, or hips, use reach. Do not add finger detail unless requested.
- For hands on knees, use move_effector right_hand to right_knee and left_hand to left_knee.
- For crouch, squat, or kneel with hands on knees, combine shift_weight down with those hand-to-knee move_effector operators. Use mild intensity for a half crouch and medium or strong intensity for a full crouch.
- If the hands are already on the knees before crouching, sequence the hand-to-knee operators first, then start shift_weight down after them so the app can hold that contact while lowering.
- Default duration is 2.5 seconds.
- Maximum duration is 5 seconds.
- For pose requests, set holdFinalPose true and loop false.
- For repeated motion, set loop true only when the whole motion should repeat.
- Use startTime and endTime only when operators should happen in sequence.
- If timing is not important, omit startTime and endTime.
- Use move_effector for hands or torso only. Torso supports forward only.
- Prefer reach over move_effector when the requested hand placement needs a specific offset from head, chest, or hips.
- Hands support forward, front_of_face, front_of_chest, chest_center, above_head, and either side_of_head region. For knees, use right_hand to right_knee, left_hand to left_knee, or either hand to knees. Do not target waist or the opposite knee.
- Use hand_shape for fist, open hand, relaxed hand, or peace sign.
- Use finger for detailed finger curling, pointing, counting, spreading, or thumb-only motion. Select four_fingers to curl the index, middle, ring, and pinky together while leaving the thumb clear; all includes the thumb.
- Use look for head/gaze direction.
- Use orient_effector only for a hand, head, or gaze facing a direction.
- Use oscillate only for hands, head, gaze, or torso when waving, shaking, nodding, swaying, trembling, or repeating side-to-side/up-down/twist motion.
- Use shift_weight for leaning, crouching, stepping weight, bracing, or lowering the body.
- For a wave, move the hand to the side of the head, open the hand, then oscillate that hand horizontally.
- For greeting, add look target viewer and a mild forward weight shift if appropriate.
- For making a fist, use hand_shape with shape fist.
- For guard, move both hands toward front_of_face/front_of_chest, use hand_shape fist, and use a mild or medium shift_weight forward. Do not shift backward for guard unless the user explicitly asks to lean back or recoil.
- For pointing, move the hand forward, curl all fingers, then open the index finger with finger curl 0.
- For crouch or squat, use shift_weight direction down.
- For bowing or leaning forward, use shift_weight direction forward.
- For looking left/right/up/down, use look with the matching target.
- If the user says strong, dramatic, battle, aggressive, or clear, use intensity strong.
- If the user says small, subtle, soft, gentle, slight, or tiny, use intensity mild.
- Otherwise use intensity medium.
